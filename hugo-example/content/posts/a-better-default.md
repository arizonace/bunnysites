Most of our days are shaped by defaults. The browser opens with a dozen tabs. Notifications arrive with a sound. Meetings take thirty minutes because thirty minutes is what the calendar suggests.

The useful question is not “what is possible?” but “what happens when I do nothing?” A good default is a small vote for the day you want to have.

## Start with the frequent moments

Choose one repeated moment: opening your laptop, beginning a task, or ending the day. Remove one distraction from that moment, then make the better option the obvious one.

> Good defaults are kindnesses you leave for your future self.

The change does not need to be dramatic. A clear desktop, a quiet phone, or a short meeting template can do more than a complicated system you will not keep.
